The Beauty Is In The Manual Ritual

Written By: Zachary Kai » Published: 27 Nov 2026 | Updated: 27 Nov 2026

Expand For Other (Hopefully Useful) Metadata
  • Issue No. 65
  • Reading Time: ~2 min (at 238 WPM)
  • Word Count: 313

They say what goes around comes around, and it’s funny how not only history repeats itself, but one’s life patterns, too. Almost ten years ago now (hard to believe, but such is the incredulity with a short-so-far existence) but I lived for a while in Tokyo in a tiny apartment. And then, further south for several months.

What, apart from the country and culture did those living arrangements have in common each day? I couldn’t make my bed, no: I had to put it away. Sure, I could’ve made it, leaving it looking nice and neat…but then I’d have no room in space to do, well, anything else really. So away the bed went each day.

And recently? I’ve had a similar situation. And at risk of sounding trite or overprivileged or insensitive…I enjoy the manual ritual of putting away everything at the start of the day.

While many people have extolled the simple virtues of making one’s bed, I’d argue the usual Japanese process takes it a step further: a true transition to mark the end of sleeping and the start of wakefulness.

Plus, for someone who’s existence is almost entirely spent online (all my work is internet-based) there’s something lovely about having an immediate sense of accomplishment.

Sure, I get that each time I finish a task for a client, or wrap up a major project, but there’s something almost carnal about seeing the physical results: it’s embodied, real, dare I say alive.

While there’s something probably more profound somewhere in that about convenience culture and being conscious about what we let be automated in our lives and how and ‘living beyond the algorithm…’

I really just wanted to post something short and sweet that’s been on my mind lately.

So take your manual rituals however you like them, however small: the savoring is the point.
