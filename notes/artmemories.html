Memories With Art, By James

Close your eyes. Picture a painting you love, or think of a piece of music you love. Where are you? What stands out? How do you feel?

I am entranced. Moments earlier, I was ready to leave the gallery, having spent time with paintings of Nature, the names of whose painters I cannot presently remember but whose works have left a strong impression in my memory. I see in my memory the time I have spent looking at the countrysides in the museum hallway, and the time I have spent seeing the looks in other people's eyes that make me think "are you falling in love with this painting too?"

When I leave galleries – assuming I am not being asked to leave by an announcement system because it is closing time – I like to meander. I intend to walk faster because I have the intent to leave – to continue on with my day, to get a coffee, to bask in the stream of life that is the busy plaza outside from which I can see Nature and humanity and architecture and hope – but inevitably something catches my eye: a painting I haven't seen in a while, a rearranged wall, a new addition to a display. I revel in those moments.

Turner's painting of Norham Castle, which I see on the way out of the National Gallery of Scotland, is transfixing. I always want to look at the painting; to savour every moment of its display. I have written about Turner's painting of Norham Castle before, but when I close my eyes it continues to be the painting I see.

I can close my eyes and see the ink of Rembrandt and the whit of Magritte and the passion of Picasso, but, in this moment, I see Turner. Maybe it is because I have seen the painting recently that it stands out so much. Whatever the reason, I sit, now, under the blanket of late evening that will soon become near-midnight and then a new day, with the impression of Turner's yellow sunrise in my mind – of the blurriness in the painting, and how it makes me want to fill in the details while also realising it is ambiguity that makes the work so beautiful.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

I close my eyes and think of a piece of music. I hear Taylor Swift's "Our Song" and "Call it What You Want," mashed up. I think of the bridge, of how much I love to sing the words passionately as I play the song on my guitar. I hear a blend of more songs on the periphery: like when I closed my eyes to think of art, many memories made their way to the forefront of my mind. I see the time spent in my chair where I write words and play music, sitting on the edge with my guitar perched on one leg, one hand on the fretboard, and the other holding a grey plectrum, ready to play. I feel ready, and excited, and full of the potential that comes with making music.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

I sit in wonder at the possibility that another person might read these words at morning or midnight, with a cup of coffee by their sides or a glass of water, with memories of daydreams or the hope of dreams near in their minds. Dear reader, this is you. What do you hear, or see – now, or when you think of art, or architecture, or music? Does a moment stand out, where you were transfixed, or left thinking, or laughed out loud because your experience was made not only by a particular artwork but by the fact you spent that day with friends who made you smile? How do you feel? Does your world feel bigger, different?
