What We Made On HTML Day 2026

Written By: Zachary Kai » Published: 9 Aug 2026 | Updated: 9 Aug 2026

Expand For Other (Hopefully Useful) Metadata
  • Reading Time: ~3 min (at 238 WPM)
  • Word Count: 650

A typography tasting menu, poems written in HTML, silk-printed t-shirts, and a collage that turned into birds.

HTML Day fell on Saturday, and I hosted two online sessions for it: one at 6pm CET, one at 6pm AEST. 60 mins, everyone muted, introductions in the chat, writing HTML in company, then showing what we'd made.

I said I'd take notes and pass on the link. Here they are: who turned up, what people meant to make, what we talked about, and what got made.

The CET Session

Who Came

  • Sophia
  • Ross

What People Planned To Make

  • A homepage for a new library site.
  • A page of one's houseplants (seconded.)
  • A page about the houseplants one had previously.
  • A twine about the frustrations of captchas.
  • A collection of 'strong opinions, loosely held.'
  • A page about the oldest settlement in one's home region.
  • A collage of elements that anyone could contribute to.
  • A podroll: a page of one's favorite podcasts.
  • Dusting off one's HTML skills, no particular destination.

What We Made

  • A 'tasting menu' for testing typography: page and repository.
  • A collage of elements, which was mostly bird-themed.
  • A search page for a library: with an under construction gif added.
  • A page of affirmations and reminders for one's self.

The AEST Session

Who Came

  • April
  • Chris
  • fLaMEd
  • James
  • Kitasenju

What People Planned To Make

  • A links hub for one's website.
  • An alternative blog post template.
  • An 'elsewhere' page.
  • A page of one's poetry.
  • A poem formatted in HTML.
  • Something written with a poetic air.
  • A blank HTML page.
  • A freewrite, if inspiration struck.

What We Talked About

  • What a 'links' or 'resources' page can look like.
  • The joys of writing HTML, especially returning to it after a while.
  • The difficulties of adding links in HTML, and the rewards: how it makes a piece more interesting, and more connected to everyone on the net.
  • Which HTML tags are best for formatting poetry.
  • A 'slashpage' to document the attempts and evolutions in one's note-taking system. Would it live at '/pkm' or '/notetaking'?

What I Made

Hosting means I write less, but I got something down:

  • A podroll.
  • A page about the houseplants I used to have.
  • A 'hills' page for the opinions I'm willing to die on.
  • A museum slashpage for the communities and creative spaces I've passed through online.
  • Made a few contribution to the bird collage.

None are ready. They'll turn up here eventually.

Thank You

To everyone who came: thank you. And to Naty for spotting errors!

Writing HTML is a fine way to spend an hour. Writing it in a quiet room full of other people doing the same is better, and I don't entirely know why.

Same time next year.
